### Round-1 findings
- **F1 (BLOCKING — INV-LEDGER-4 failed OPEN):** the deferred non-negativity trigger functions were not `SECURITY DEFINER`, so their verification SELECT ran as the caller and was **filtered by RLS** — clearing or switching `app.household_id` before COMMIT made the row invisible (`FOUND=false`) and a **negative quantity committed silently** (repro: −40000000 committed; owner-context control correctly raised). The only enforcement layer for this invariant, disarmable by a pooled-session context reset.
- **F2 (REQUIRED):** the sequence-disclosure oracle the worker had reported closed survived in another layer — `UNIQUE(item_id, sequence)` was not household-scoped, and unique indexes fire before RI, so probing a foreign item with ascending sequences flipped duplicate-key→FK exactly at the victim's ledger length.
- **F3 (latent):** a trigger error message echoed another tenant's UUID (unreachable today, one schema change from reachable). **F4:** the read path fetched snapshot columns and ignored them — forced drift was silently accepted. **F5:** "rehydrate is the corruption detector" was true (reviewer verified manually: all three corruptions refused) but untested. **F6–F10 (LOW/INFO):** concurrency loser fails `23505` not `40001` (retry-helper spec must cover both); migration-runner doc/blast-radius nits; unpinned postgres image; lot-instant canonicalisation; pure helpers gated behind DATABASE_URL.

## Standing rules this ticket demonstrated (recorded into ADR-003 at acceptance)
1. **RLS does not protect against unique-index oracles** — household-scope every unique constraint on tenant tables.
2. **A non-`SECURITY DEFINER` trigger that reads household-scoped tables fails OPEN under RLS** — definer + pinned search_path is mandatory for such triggers.
