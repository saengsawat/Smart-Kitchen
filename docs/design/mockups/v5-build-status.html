Proposed milestones

These are discussion checkpoints, not new engineering commitments. The detailed work plan remains in the project backlog.

1 · Dean’s guided development test

Timing: arrange the next walkthrough.

Try inventory, barcode scanning, and existing shopping operations together using the local development setup. Sample identities and missing flows will be clearly called out.

2 · Remote test environment

Timing: after vendor/account decisions and deployment work.

Choose team-controlled hosting/auth projects, deploy the API and database, secure access, and arrange phone-build distribution. Signing up for hosting alone does not make the mobile app remotely testable.

3 · Friends MVP beta

Target: around February 2027, subject to agreed scope and available development time.

Connect the core loop: pantry → meal suggestions → missing ingredients → shopping → cooking → updated pantry. Add real accounts, clear allergy limitations, and reliable data handling before inviting friends.

4 · Paid public launch

Timing: decide after beta results; no date promised.

Resolve beta issues, validate willingness to pay, and complete security/privacy, costs/support, purchase access, and store/distribution requirements.

Decisions for our next meeting

  • First-beta scope: agree which v5 features are essential and which stay later. New prototype features can change the workload.
  • Vendor account ownership: agree the team/business email or domain, account/project owner, billing responsibility, and individual admin access with MFA. Avoid sharing a personal password. While unincorporated, document who holds each account and the intended transfer.
  • Hosting and auth: choose vendors, check ownership/transfer rules, and create the projects together. Prefer a stable project that can move from free to paid; a billing upgrade is different from migrating to another vendor.
  • Spending: agree a monthly budget, usage alerts, and who approves paid services. Add an LLM account when the relevant feature is ready to integrate.
  • Product decisions: settle meal-plan scope and household permissions, which affect the held Menu/Home and server-side restriction work.
Why no completion percentage? A screen can be visible while its real data, AI, failure handling, and security are unfinished. Feature checkpoints are a more useful progress measure than “20% done.”
