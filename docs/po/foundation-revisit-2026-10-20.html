1. The short version

  • What stays as is. The append-only inventory ledger, Postgres row-level security and default-deny authorization, the deterministic allergen engine, exact unit conversion, the provider ports with fixture adapters, idempotency keys on every write. These are tested and nothing in Dean's document asks them to change. DOCUMENTED
  • What is missing. The menu loop (plan, then shop for the gap, then cook, then the pantry goes down) joins recipes to the pantry on one thing: which ingredient is this. Nothing in the built app answers that today. The domain model planned a CanonicalIngredient list in September and the schema has an ingredient_ref column on inventory items, but no taxonomy exists and nothing fills the column. That is the centre of this revisit. DOCUMENTED
  • Recipes come from a small catalogue we own, drafted by AI offline and reviewed by a person, behind a port so other sources can plug in later. Spoonacular's terms rule it out as the store for a plan (section 5). PROPOSED
  • Plan shopping rows become a computed view (plan need minus what is on hand), so "auto-add" is true by construction and can never duplicate. PROPOSED
  • The e2e browser check goes into CI as part of the foundation, because the menu loop is the most multi-screen flow yet. PROPOSED

2. How the pieces connect

Why the centre box matters: every arrow in the loop needs it. "Need 2 lb chicken" (plan) minus "have 1.25 lb" (pantry) is only computable if both sides agree the thing is chicken breast. Cooking can only take chicken out of the pantry if it knows which pantry items are chicken. Without the list, each of those features would invent its own matching, and they would disagree. PROPOSED

3. The seven foundation pieces

3.1 Ingredient identity NEW

Today. inventory_items.ingredient_ref exists (text, nullable, writable by the app role per migration 0006) and is read and written by the repository, but nothing sets it. Items are a barcode product (product_ref) or a typed name. domain-model.md §2 rule 2 already says "Product ≠ Ingredient" and lists CanonicalIngredient as "small curated taxonomy to start". DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • A curated ingredient list as versioned data in the repo, sized by what the catalogue needs (a few hundred keys at most to start). Each entry: a stable key (chicken-breast), a label, a shopping group (Produce, Dairy...), a default unit kind (mass, volume or count), the allergen codes it contains, diet tags (meat, poultry, pork, fish, shellfish, dairy, egg, alcohol, gluten-grain...), and optional unit bridges (3.3).
  • A migration loads it into a global read-only table so SQL can join on it (no household data in it; the app role gets SELECT only).
  • Pantry items get mapped to a key: suggested from the Open Food Facts category on scan, picked from a type-ahead on manual add, and settable on the item page for existing items. A suggested mapping is Estimated until the user confirms it, the same tier rules as everything else.
  • An item with no mapping is honest: it does not count toward a recipe and the screens say "not matched", never a guess.

Why first. Pieces 3.3 to 3.5 all join on it. Risk class: schema change, Opus review (rule 23). Migration: yes (rule 16, explained before running).

3.2 Recipe catalogue behind a port NEW

Today. No recipes exist outside the prototype's fixtures. D-015 says "AI-original recipes for MVP" and is PROPOSED; Q6 (AI only, or license a database) is open; D-017 gate b (who may mint a Known Fact allergen completeness declaration) is open. DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • A RecipeSourcePort in packages/adapters, like the product lookup port. First adapter: our own catalogue, stored as schema-validated data files and loaded into global read-only tables (recipes, recipe_ingredients).
  • Each recipe: title, servings, time, steps (temperatures stored in °C, shown in the household's system), ingredients (ingredient key, exact quantity in micros, unit, optional flag, the display line), calories and macros per serving (Estimated, with source), image per D-032, and provenance: drafted by (model and date), reviewed by (person and date).
  • Diet tags on a recipe are computed from its ingredients' tags (deterministic), not typed by hand.
  • Authoring: an AI drafts recipes offline in a session, a person reviews each against a checklist, the reviewed file is committed. Same pattern as D-032 food images. No LLM at runtime, no spend, no quota.
  • Size: 20 to 30 recipes is enough for internal testing. The beta size is OPEN.
  • Later sources (a licensed API, runtime AI) become more adapters. Their recipes enter as proposals at Estimated tier (rule 8) and can never license "no known allergen match".

Allergen trust. OPEN This is the safety call in the revisit. A reviewed ingredient list is the only way a recipe can ever read "no known allergen match" (D-017 gate b). Options: (a) a two-person review (Andy and Dean) against a written checklist mints the declaration, risk accepted and recorded; (b) no declaration in the beta, so every recipe shows the amber "allergen data incomplete" line until a qualified reviewer signs off. Architect leans (a) for the friends beta with the checklist in the repo, because (b) puts an amber warning on every recipe and trains people to ignore it. Copy never says "safe" in either case (SR-1, SR-2).

Risk class: allergen-adjacent, Opus review.

3.3 Unit bridges NEW

Today. Same-kind conversion is exact (M1-T3). Cross-kind conversion (cups of rice to pounds) needs a caller-supplied bridge and otherwise refuses with INCOMPATIBLE_UNITS, never a guess. Where bridge data lives was deferred in OQ-2 ("still open, deferred to M2"). DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • Bridges live on the ingredient list entry: density for volume and mass, grams per piece for count and mass ("1 medium onion ≈ 150 g"). Always Estimated tier.
  • Recipes are authored in the ingredient's default unit kind wherever a cook would accept it, so most lines never need a bridge.
  • A quantity converted through a bridge is shown and stored as Estimated (gap rows and cook deductions say "Est.").
  • No bridge: the line is listed as "not counted" in the gap and "not deducted" when cooking.

Risk class: inventory arithmetic, Opus.

3.4 Plan shopping rows as a computed view NEW

Today. shopping_rows (migration 0009) stores rows with origin_kind limited to 'member'; a row may link to one inventory item; GET /v1/shopping computes the gap from that item's snapshot with the domain's neededQuantity. BUG-006 taught that a bought row must show the bought amount from the ledger, not a gap recomputed to 0. DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • Plan-derived rows are not stored. Each read computes, per ingredient key, over the plan entries in the window: need = sum of recipe quantity × (servings ÷ recipe servings), converted to one unit; on hand = sum of usable stock in items mapped to that key; show the row when need − on hand > 0 (INV-SHOP-1 applied after aggregation).
  • User state (ticked, bought) is kept per (household, ingredient key) in an append-only state table. A ticked or bought row never disappears on its own (Dean MP-41). Bought amounts come from the PURCHASE ledger rows (the BUG-006 pattern).
  • Typed-in rows stay stored rows and gain an optional ingredient key. If a typed-in row and a plan need share a key, the list shows one row with both labels ("From plan" and "Added by you"), as Dean's MP-45 asks.
  • Consequences: "auto-add" is true by construction, re-running can never duplicate a row (Dean's §13 idempotency rule), and the three sync modes are not needed. The current "Add missing" button goes away.

Risk class: shopping-gap math (INV-SHOP-1), Opus. Migration: yes, on M7-T1's table.

3.5 Cooking deducts from the pantry NEW

Today. The ledger already has the USE_IN_MEAL type (a decrease), the FEFO lot planner (M1-T8), idempotent writes and compensating-row undo. PRD §4 has meal logging and automatic decrement as fast-follow; the prototype's cook sheet already promises it. Andy, 2026-10-20: "let's get it work". DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • One cook action = one idempotency key = one transaction: a meal_logs row (append-only) plus one USE_IN_MEAL row per deducted lot, each carrying the meal log id as its correlation.
  • Per recipe line: find items mapped to the ingredient key, convert (bridge if needed), allocate FEFO across those items' lots.
  • Shortfall policy: deduct what is on hand, record the shortfall on the meal log, never over-consume. This closes OQ-1 and A9 if Andy agrees.
  • Unmatched or unbridgeable lines are listed "not deducted" on the confirm sheet. The person can edit amounts before confirming.
  • Undo uses compensating rows, as today. The correction-rate metric gets its best signal here.

Risk class: ledger and concurrency, Opus implementation and review, architect code pass.

3.6 Plan entries and household settings NEW

Proposal. PROPOSED

  • No "plan" object. A plan is just entries keyed by (household, date, meal). The horizon (1 to 7 days) is a window over them. Shrinking the window hides entries without deleting them, which gives Dean's "trimmed entries go to history" for free.
  • Entry: date, meal (breakfast, lunch, dinner), type (recipe or custom text), recipe id or custom name, servings, status (planned, cooked, skipped), source (manual or suggested), updated by, updated at. One entry per cell, last write wins with "updated by {initials}" shown (ARCHITECTURE §4 already sets last-write-wins for CRUD rows).
  • Custom text entries are free text: never screened, never on the shopping list, and the screen says so (Dean MP-15). Free text is untrusted data (PRD §11).
  • Household settings (one row per household): unit system, plan length (default 5, max 7), meals shown, diners per meal (default household size), diet profile (household default), calorie goal (off by default). Who may edit them depends on A3.
  • Ranking runs on the server and is deterministic (no LLM): uses expiring stock, number of items to buy, diet fit, time. Favourites and history join when they exist.

Risk class: plan API Opus (idempotency, tenancy); settings Sonnet.

3.7 The e2e browser gate in CI NEW

Today. apps/mobile/scripts/web-demo-smoke.mjs drives headless Chrome through onboarding with no extra packages. CI only checks that the app builds (expo export); the smoke script runs by hand at acceptance. Andy's device re-test list in STATUS has grown since 2026-10-01. DOCUMENTED

Proposal. PROPOSED Run the script in the CI quality job on every push, then extend it to the three make-or-break flows (onboarding, scan and add, shopping close-the-loop). The menu loop (plan, shop, cook, pantry down) becomes the fourth flow when it exists. Later, a second level against the real API and the CI Postgres. This does not replace device passes (camera, Android layout).

Risk class: tooling, Sonnet.

5. Spoonacular, checked

Fetched 2026-10-20 from spoonacular.com/food-api/terms and spoonacular.com/food-api/pricing. Re-check before relying on these; terms change. DOCUMENTED (as fetched)

  • Storage: caching up to 1 hour, with prior written permission ("After 1 hour, you must delete your cache and refresh the data via the spoonacular API"). Only the recipe id, title and image URL may be stored indefinitely; "you may not store any other data from the spoonacular API".
  • Free plan: 50 points a day, then no more calls; 1 request per second; backlink required.
  • Use: "you cannot use spoonacular data to create a site or application meant to provide the same experience as spoonacular" (Spoonacular offers its own meal planner).
  • Disclaimers: allergy, cost and nutrition disclaimers are the app's responsibility.

What that means for the foundation. PROPOSED A plan holds recipes for days; the gap, the cook sheet and the allergen screen all need the ingredient lists for that whole time. With a 1-hour storage limit every plan view re-fetches, and 50 points a day runs out after a few views of a 7-day plan. Their ingredient data is third-party, so it can only be Estimated and can never license "no known allergen match". Recommendation: do not use Spoonacular, even for testing. The owned catalogue (3.2) is free, has no quota, and is the long-term path anyway.

7. Decisions for Andy, in one pass

Each is a yes, a no, or a change. Nothing is written to DECISIONS.md until Andy answers.

  1. D1 Recipe source. Owned, reviewed catalogue behind RecipeSourcePort; no Spoonacular; runtime AI later. (Amends D-015, closes Q6.)
  2. D2 Allergen trust root for catalogue recipes (D-017 gate b). (a) Andy and Dean two-person review against a checklist mints the declaration, risk recorded; or (b) no declaration until a qualified reviewer, every recipe amber. Architect leans (a) for the friends beta. OPEN
  3. D3 Ingredient identity. Curated ingredient list as repo data loaded into a read-only table; pantry items mapped by suggestion plus user confirmation; unmapped items never guessed.
  4. D4 Unit bridges. On the ingredient entry, always Estimated; recipes authored in default unit kinds; no bridge means not counted and not deducted. (Closes OQ-2's open half.)
  5. D5 Shopping. Plan rows computed (need − on hand per ingredient), per-ingredient ticked and bought state, typed-in rows stay stored and gain an optional ingredient key, one row per ingredient with every origin label. Migration on M7-T1's table.
  6. D6 Cooking deducts in MVP. Meal log plus USE_IN_MEAL rows under one key; shortfall policy: deduct what is on hand, flag the rest. (Closes OQ-1, A9.)
  7. D7 Plan. Grid of entries by date and meal, 1 to 7 days (default 5), breakfast, lunch, dinner, add, swap, clear, cook; last write wins with "updated by"; no plan object. Custom slots, copy, move, drag and auto-fill after the beta.
  8. D8 Diets. Dean's v3 list of 8; exclusion diets hide, nutrient diets rank until signed off; gluten-free stays an allergy. (Amends D-031.)
  9. D9 Unit default. US or device locale. OPEN
  10. D10 Calorie goal. In the beta or right after. OPEN
  11. D11 e2e gate. Smoke script in CI now; three flows; the menu loop as the fourth.
  12. D12 A3, household permissions. Still blocks server-side allergies, which the beta needs. The decision-brief recommendation stands: the owner enters for everyone at signup, members edit their own, allergies visible to the household, profiles private. OPEN
